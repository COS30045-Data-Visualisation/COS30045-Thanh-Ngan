Televisions

Televisions are among the most common appliances in Australian homes, and they are often left on for hours every day. This page looks at how much electricity different types of television typically use.

Why Televisions Matter

A single television uses less power than an air conditioner or a water heater, but it runs for long periods, often alongside a set top box, a games console or a sound bar. Over a full year, this adds up to a noticeable part of a household's electricity use.

Long viewing hours

Televisions are often on for several hours every day, so even a modest wattage adds up over a full year.

Brightness

Higher brightness levels consume more electricity. Display settings used in stores are usually brighter than what is necessary for a comfortable home environment.

Screen size

Screen sizes keep growing, and a larger panel generally draws more power than a smaller one.

Extra devices

Set top boxes, games consoles and sound bars are often left running alongside the television.

Panel technology

LED, OLED, and LCD(LED) displays can have different energy consumption patterns, even when they are the same size.

Buying decisions

Comparing star ratings before you buy can lower running costs for the whole life of the television.
